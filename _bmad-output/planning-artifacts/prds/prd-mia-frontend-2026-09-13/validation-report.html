MIA Frontend PRD Foundation — Validation Report

_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/ux-designs/ux-mia-frontend-2026-09-12/FRONTEND-PRD-FOUNDATION.md
Poor

This document is unusually honest about what it excludes and unusually rigorous about security, session lifecycle, localization, and accessibility bounds — those sections are genuinely buildable, and all seven delegated companion contracts resolve. It fails as a PRD spine on three load-bearing axes: it carries no requirement identifiers, no success metrics, and no performance requirements, so neither "done for this requirement" nor "successful at release" can be answered from it. The core defect is an inversion: the implementation policy it delegates elsewhere is dense and precise, while the product commitments only a PRD can own — measurable outcomes, a cut line, bounds on the surfaces the thesis bets on — are the thinnest content in the file.

The three extra reviewers materially widen the picture rather than confirming it. The adversarial reviewer found that both flagship outcomes are not merely unmeasured but structurally unobservable — telemetry is excluded (L301) and the only instrument is one developer's Playwright run on macOS (L436) — and surfaced two product-level silences the rubric does not reach: safeguarding for minors is a disclaimer (L299) composed with supervisors blinded to active chat (L184), and guardian consent, age verification, and data export are excluded in a single unreasoned bullet (L85) while four EU locales ship at launch (L362). It also found a genuine internal contradiction: the raw-error-body mandate (L262) cannot be satisfied without breaching the document's own L289/L293/L296 rules, and L272 forbids the frontend from mitigating. The source-fidelity reviewer confirmed every declared source and cross-reference resolves and that the document does not invent transport — its defects there are omission, chiefly that SSE establishment resets the server idle timer (product-requirements.md:530-532) while the idle model here is built purely on user-initiated requests, plus four confirmed tutoring-lifecycle rules and login throttling that never reach the page. The downstream reviewer confirmed bmad-architecture can proceed but bmad-create-epics-and-stories cannot cut a value epic, and found ownership asserted at L256 for two concerns the named owner does not contain.

On the grade. No dimension is broken; four of seven are strong or adequate. The Poor grade is produced mechanically by the presence of critical findings, and should be read as "critical gaps block use as a PRD spine", not as "low-quality document". Nine distinct critical findings survive de-duplication across the four reviewers.

Decision-readiness

adequate

The document states real decisions as decisions rather than burying them. "Exact raw body disclosure is an approved Product Owner decision and must not be silently replaced with a validated-field subset" (§ Error Response Bodies, 270–271) names the decision, its owner, and forecloses the tempting alternative in one sentence. Similar quality appears at "Product Owner Safari evidence is mandatory" (421), "Firefox is unsupported but not proactively blocked" (422), and the refusal to overclaim at 428. None of this is smoothed-to-neutral writing.

What a decision-maker cannot do with this document is decide anything about delivery. Release Priorities (461–479) looks like a prioritization but closes with "Priority expresses UX refinement and delivery sequence, not permission to omit confirmed release-one behavior" (479) — which removes the only lever priorities exist to provide. Every included surface is release-one, WCAG 2.2 AA is release-blocking (381), seven locales ship at launch (362), and multiple backend blockers are hard release gates. A sponsor facing a date slip finds no decision framework here at all.

Open Questions (535–538) reinforces this: two questions, both tooling selection. Not one open product tension, despite launch stakes and seventeen tracked backend blockers.

Strategic coherence

thin

There is a real thesis and the priorities follow it, which is the half that works. The objective names "the two highest-priority experiences" (50–51), the scope section deliberately downgrades everything else (80–81), and Priority 1 (469–473) contains exactly the thesis-serving items. That arc is coherent and the document is not a backlog with headings.

The half that fails is validation. There is no Success Metrics section anywhere — "metric" appears twice, both incidental. The closest outcome claim is "Improved marks remain an intended benefit, not a guarantee" (57), which explicitly declines to be measured. Meanwhile the document excludes the entire measurement apparatus (88, 301). Those exclusions are defensible for a platform handling minors' data; the failure is that the document never connects the two facts. Having chosen a thesis that is a behavioral claim about students, and having forbidden every instrument that could observe student behavior, it owes the reader an explicit statement of how success will be judged instead. It says nothing, so the thesis is unfalsifiable by accident rather than by decision.

Prioritized Experience Outcomes (111–121) is the structural substitute and cannot carry the load: each item is a capability assertion with an unmeasurable qualifier. No counter-metrics exist, which matters more than usual here — the tutoring thesis has an obvious failure mode (students completing sessions faster by disengaging) that nothing in the document guards against.

critical

No Success Metrics and no measurement story

absent entirely; cf. 57, 88, 301
The PRD bets on a behavioral outcome ("a student can complete a second tutoring session independently", 51–52) while excluding analytics, telemetry, and session replay, and never states the alternative evidence path. Nobody downstream can judge whether release one succeeded. Corroborated by the adversarial and downstream reviewers, who add that the only instrument named anywhere is one developer's Playwright run on macOS (436).
Fix: add a Success Metrics section stating how each Prioritized Experience Outcome will be evidenced without telemetry — named pilot cohort, supervisor-reported activation time, backend aggregate session counts — or record an explicit Product Owner decision that release one ships unmeasured, with a named accountable owner for discovering failure.
high

Prioritized Experience Outcomes have no pass/fail definition

§ Prioritized Experience Outcomes, 113–121
"resolve it, and activate confidently" (113–114) and "without human interface guidance" (115) cannot be adjudicated. These are the document's stated top two priorities, so their unfalsifiability propagates into every story derived from them.
Fix: restate each outcome with an observable condition — e.g. activation completes with every unmet prerequisite reachable from the hub in one hop and no external documentation reference — or convert to a moderated usability acceptance gate with a named population, task, and pass threshold.

Done-ness clarity

adequate

The density of genuinely testable bounds in the middle of this document is well above average. "Transport loss schedules 1, 2, and 4 second reconnect-availability delays" (250), "five-failure invalidation" (340), "MFA code entry uses one native text input holding the full pasteable value" (134), and "320 effective CSS pixels and at 1280 CSS pixels with 400% zoom, in Standard, Large, and Extra large text modes" (401–403) are all conditions an engineer can write a failing test against today. Negative requirements are equally sharp: "Lifecycle events and transport loss never open an authenticated request or replacement EventSource" (240) is directly assertable in a network trace.

Two gaps undercut this. The first is total: there is not one performance requirement in 538 lines. The document itself establishes the need — "sometimes on shared devices and weak connections" (61) — then converts it exclusively into resilience requirements. Every other quality family gets a release-blocking gate; frontend performance has no gate, no budget, and no owner, on an SSE-streaming product whose primary user is on a school network.

The second gap is pointed: the surfaces the thesis stakes everything on are the least bounded prose in the file. "a calm Home continuation surface" (150–151) is the entire specification of the landing surface for every authenticated user.

critical

No performance requirements anywhere

§ Desktop and Platform Scope, 399–428; § Test and Verification Model, 430–459
Despite "weak connections" (61) and an SSE-streaming core (248–254), no latency, render, bundle, or throughput bound exists, and the verification model contains no performance test category alongside its accessibility, localization, security, and resilience categories. The adversarial reviewer adds that a self-hosted product also states no deployment envelope it was designed against.
Fix: add a performance section with product-specific bounds — authenticated shell first render on a throttled school-network profile, streamed-token render latency, transcript render cost at a stated turn count — the observable states for exceeding them, and the matching automated test category.
high

Refinement tiering is unfalsifiable

§ Included, 80–81; § Prioritized Experience Outcomes, 121
"highest UX refinement", "visually conventional and merely functional", and "conventional, consistent, unambiguous flows" give a reviewer no basis to accept or reject. Since this tiering is the document's main scope-shaping device, its vagueness is load-bearing — and it is the only quality bar for the entire mentoring and administration surface, so any implementation passes.
Fix: define each tier by observable obligations — e.g. refined tier requires an empty, loading, partial, error, and recovery state per surface plus a keyboard walkthrough; functional tier requires the standard states, unmodified inherited blueprints, and no bespoke layout work.

Scope honesty

strong

This is the document's best dimension by a clear margin. The Excluded section (83–92) does real work: eight dense bullets that pre-empt exactly the omissions a reader would otherwise assume were oversights. Deferred Backlog (527–533) separately distinguishes deferred from excluded, including the conditional mascot entry (532).

The document is also honest about its own incompleteness in ways that are easy to omit. "The licensed Tailwind Plus inventory is not yet bound… the handoff does not claim exact references already exist" (415–417) volunteers a weakness rather than papering over it. "Silence is unresolved, not permission to assume" (45–46) and the OpenAPI blocking rule (277–279) convert scope honesty into an enforceable working rule. Open-items density is appropriate: unresolved work is concentrated in a named, owned ledger rather than scattered as hedges.

The adversarial reviewer contests the outer edge of this dimension: several exclusions (guardian consent, age verification, data export, safeguarding alerts) are stated without rationale or a named risk acceptor, which is scope honesty about the what but not about the consequence. Those are carried as separate critical findings below rather than downgrading this verdict.

Downstream usability

thin

This dimension matters most here, because the document is explicitly chain-top. Delegation itself is handled well — all seven companion contracts exist in the same folder, ownership is stated per companion, and every identifier checked resolves, including BCG-001, BCG-014, BCG-015, BCG-016, BCG-017, and backend AS-1 through AS-32. That is responsible delegation, not evasion.

What blocks extraction is that the document has no identifiers of its own. "Functional Requirement Families" (123–219) is nine prose subsections; there is no FR-* anywhere in the file, and REQUIREMENT-FLOW-MATRIX.md maps backend FR-1..FR-84, so frontend requirement identity does not exist in this folder at all. Nothing in this document can be referenced, versioned, diffed, or marked done.

The numbering that does exist creates a collision: line 483 instructs authors to derive from backend AS-1..AS-32 and the following list is numbered 1 through 16. Four protagonists (Pablo, Elena, Rosa, Diego) appear only inside that list with no introduction. And there is no Glossary, in a document whose precision depends on invented terms of art — "privacy shield", "security epoch", "revalidation needed", "restricted stage", "stable-reading mode", plus the load-bearing readiness/approval and brief/briefing pairs.

§ Functional Requirement Families, 123–219; document-wide
Nine prose subsections with no FR-* or equivalent, and no frontend requirement IDs in the delegated matrix either — its FR-1..FR-84 are explicitly backend IDs (REQUIREMENT-FLOW-MATRIX.md:21-22). Shape fit

adequate

The chosen shape is defensible. For a capability-dense, multi-stakeholder platform whose journeys and per-surface states already live in adjacent owned contracts, a requirement-family narrative plus explicit delegation is the right call. Declaring itself a "handoff companion for a future frontend PRD" (17) and carrying status: draft is consistent with having no metrics section on a first pass. The absence of inline user journeys is correct, not under-formalization.

The shape problem is an inversion rather than a wrong template. The two longest sections — State and Integration Contract (221–283) and Security, Privacy, and Safety (285–358) — are dense normative implementation policy at a granularity no PRD needs, while the sections only a PRD can own occupy about twenty lines. The result reads closer to an engineering policy manual with a PRD preamble than to a PRD foundation.

That inversion also produces a self-consistency problem: the document forbids duplicating delegated contracts (22–23), then duplicates them at length. Two normative statements of the same rule in two owned documents is exactly the divergence risk the delegation was meant to prevent, and the repository's own one-source-of-truth rule is the standard being breached.

high

Shape inversion between policy and product

§ 221–283 and § 285–358 versus § 111–121 and § 461–479
Roughly a third of the document specifies transport and lifecycle mechanics it delegates elsewhere anyway, while the product commitments that have no other owner occupy about twenty lines. Downstream architecture gets more than it needs and downstream product work gets less.
Fix: compress the mechanics sections to the decisions and their owners, moving step-level rules into the state-machine and security companions, and reinvest the space in outcomes, metrics, and the release cut line.
high

No precedence rule between this document and the seven contracts it delegates to

§ Purpose 22–29; § Authority and Sources 33–42
Line 42 ranks only "The three handoff documents win on conflict" over imports. The seven companions get ad-hoc per-topic ownership sentences while this document restates large parts of state, security, and accessibility behavior in compressed prose (221–358). When § State and Integration Contract and FRONTEND-STATE-MACHINES.md drift — and at this density they will — nothing says which wins. The master ownership sentence (25–29) is additionally seven-to-five with no pairing, and omits security and accessibility from its list of owned concerns.
Fix: add one precedence table with one row per companion and one owned concern set per row, and either demote the restatements here to explicitly non-normative summaries or delete them.

Adversarial review — 5 critical, 15 high, 9 medium, 3 low

review-adversarial.md

A disciplined constraint catalogue wearing a PRD's clothes, and the disguise is the problem: it names two "highest-priority experiences" and then builds a verification model that structurally cannot observe either of them, before or after launch. Its one real product bet — that exposing backend truth and ambiguity always beats resolving it for the user — is never stated as a bet, never defended, and never reconciled with its own user context of children on weak school connections. Strip the prose and what remains is a 538-line promise that somebody else will make the hard calls.

critical

The two priority outcomes are unmeasurable, and nothing would notice if both were missed

L50–54, L113–115, L432–456
No success rate, sample, task-completion criterion, time bound, definition of "second session", usability study, pilot, beta cohort, or post-release signal. The only instrument is L436 — "Playwright's bundled Chromium on developer macOS covers the four named journeys" — a script written by the team that knows the product, which proves a robot with perfect knowledge can click through. L301 removes the last feedback channel. If both flagship outcomes fail in every school that installs MIA, this document contains no mechanism by which anyone would ever find out.
Fix: convert each priority outcome into a pre-release moderated usability acceptance gate with a named population, task, assisted/unassisted criterion, and pass threshold; and define one privacy-preserving, operator-visible, locally-stored success signal a school can read back. If telemetry stays excluded, say explicitly who is accountable for discovering failure and how.
critical

The raw-error-body mandate contradicts three of the document's own security rules

§ Error Response Bodies L262–272 vs § Security L288–296
L262 requires "the exact HTTP status and complete raw backend response body". L289 forbids exposing provider payloads and unnecessary personal data; L293 forbids forbidden fields entering DOM, memory, or the accessibility tree; L296 forbids revealing whether an out-of-scope resource exists. A backend body embedding a provider payload, another student's name, or a distinguishing not-found detail satisfies L262 and violates all three. L268's escape hatch regulates only what the frontend adds, and L272 closes the last door: "failure never authorizes frontend redaction". The client must display whatever arrives, is banned from mitigating, and the whole safety of the feature rests on an open blocker in another repository.
Fix: state the precedence explicitly — until the error-body blocker lands, the disclosure renders a contract-bounded safe body and verbatim mode is dark. If the Product Owner truly accepts verbatim-always, record that acceptance, its data-protection rationale, and its approver next to L270.
critical

Priority 0 is hostage to another repository with no authority, escalation, date, or descope plan

§ Release Priorities L463–467; § Backend and OpenAPI Dependencies L507–525
P0 is "Accepted backend-gap closure" plus OpenAPI integration, gated by seventeen named blockers. L281 asserts BACKEND-CONTRACT-GAPS.md "is the authoritative inventory and release gate" — but a frontend planning artifact cannot bind a separate team's roadmap, and nowhere does this document name a backend counterpart, an agreed date, a negotiated priority order, an escalation route, or what ships if a blocker is declined. L278–279 is the correct engineering rule and a non-answer as a delivery plan.
Fix: add a dependency section with, per blocker, the backend owner, the target date, the frontend slices it holds, and the explicit descope decision if it slips past a stated cutoff.
critical

Safeguarding for minors is a disclaimer, not a design, and the only adults present are blinded

§ Security L299–300; § Students and Oversight L184; § Users and Context L69–70
"MIA is not an emergency service, sends no safeguarding alert, and never implies human monitoring or notification" (L299) is a legal posture, not a product answer. Compose it with "Active chat content remains hidden from supervisors until completion" (L184) and "MIA treats a student as working alone" (L69–70): a minor discloses self-harm or abuse to an AI tutor, in a product sold to schools, and by design no adult can see it while it is happening and the system will not tell anyone. That may be the correct release-one call, but a school administrator will ask it in the first ten minutes and this document does not acknowledge the question exists, names no risk acceptor, specifies no student-facing expectation-setting copy, and offers no "here is who to talk to" affordance.
Fix: add an explicit safeguarding posture section: what the student is told and when, what static help affordance exists, what the supervisor is told about their blindness to active chats, and the named person who accepted the no-alert decision.
critical

No data-protection posture for minors' data across four EU launch locales

§ Frontend Scope and Exclusions L85; § Localization L362; § Users and Context L61–62
L85 excludes, in one bullet with no rationale: "No public sign-up, operator command UI, guardian account, age verification, guardian consent, or data export." L62: "Release one uses no age differentiation." L362 launches de-DE, fr-FR, pt-PT, es-ES. GDPR Art. 8 and Arts. 15/20 are not features you scope out of a frontend with a dash — the excluded items are the named legal instruments. The document also ships no privacy-notice surface, no terms surface, no retention statement, no consent record, no DPIA reference, and no student-visible "what MIA stores about me".
Fix: add a data-protection section stating which obligations the operator owns, which the product owns, which frontend surfaces exist in release one, and who accepted the exclusions. If the answer is "the self-hosting school is the controller and carries all of it", write that sentence.

The stated user context defeats the stated transport rule, and the document never notices

§ Users and Context L61 vs § Tutoring Transport L251–252
On the exact network the document says students are on ("weak connections", L61), the no-automatic-reconnect rule (L251–252) turns every blip into a manual task for a child mid-explanation, several times per session — while the flagship outcome is that the same child completes a session unaided. high

SSE is mandated while the network it says it runs on is assumed away

Source-fidelity review — 1 critical, 2 high, 5 medium, 3 low

review-source-fidelity.md

critical

SSE establishment resets the server idle timer, and the idle-warning model never says so

frontend 250–254, 318–322; product-requirements.md:530-532, api.md:122
Backend, verbatim: "Each successful authenticated HTTP request, including establishment of an SSE connection, resets the inactivity timer." Frontend: "Each tab's idle-warning baseline is its own last successful user-initiated authenticated request" (318–319). The client model is internally sound but never states the consequence: in the flow it designates highest priority, opening or reopening the stream extends the server deadline while the client baseline does not move, so the local warning can fire against a deadline the server already pushed out — and a long-lived stream plus one explicit Try now is a keepalive the backend honors. The project's own BACKEND-CONTRACT-GAPS.md:70-72 already records this fact, so the foundation under-claims what its own ledger holds, on the single invariant AGENTS.md states most emphatically.
Fix: in § Tutoring Transport, state that SSE establishment is an authenticated request that refreshes the server-side idle deadline today, that the client baseline is deliberately more conservative, and that reconciling the two is part of the BCG-001 deadline contract.

Login throttling is confirmed backend behavior and appears nowhere

frontend 125–146, 83–92; product-requirements.md:1609-1617, paths/auth.yaml:26-28
Backend: five failed attempts per normalized username, 30 per source IP in a rolling 15-minute window, 1/2/4-second retry delays, 429 with Retry-After, and a block at the fifth username failure. OpenAPI makes it a first-class login response. The frontend enumerates the authentication family in fine detail — password managers, autofill, paste, autocomplete purposes, reveal state, MFA input shape, TOTP enrollment — and never mentions a throttled state. It is also absent from § Excluded and the acceptance scenarios, so a reader cannot tell whether it was scoped out or overlooked. The backend explicitly flags "classroom-scale bursts can temporarily throttle logins" against the exact population described at frontend line 61.
Fix: add throttled/blocked to the authentication state list, and state that the UI surfaces a wait derived only from Retry-After and never a client-computed countdown.

Four confirmed tutoring-session lifecycle rules that constrain interface affordances are absent

frontend 186–200, 83–92; product-requirements.md:1107-1113, 1186
